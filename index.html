The problem

You finished treatment. Now what?

Brain tumors are the leading cause of cancer death in young people aged 15 to 39. But for survivors, the hardest part often starts after treatment ends: brain fog, lost friendships, career uncertainty, and a healthcare system that wasn't built for you.

Pediatric care ends. Adult oncology doesn't know your history. And suddenly you're on your own — navigating independence while managing cognitive effects most people your age will never understand.

A2A exists because that gap shouldn't define your life.

What we do

Research, tools, and community — built for survivors, not systems.

A2A bridges the gap between the care you had and the life you're building. We're a research program, a clinical resource, and a peer community — all designed with and for young adults who've been through it.

Transition science

We build evidence-based tools for the handoff from pediatric to adult care — because no survivor should fall through the cracks at 18 just because the system wasn't ready.

Neurocognition & quality of life

Longitudinal research on memory, processing speed, and daily function — up to 20 years after diagnosis. We study what matters: not just survival, but how well you can live.

Peer-led support

Support groups designed by young adults, for young adults. No clinical scripts. No toxic positivity. Just real people who get it — because sometimes the most healing thing is someone who's been there.

One life at a time

This is what it looks like when the gap closes.

Behind every data point is a person rebuilding their life. These moments are why A2A exists.

The transition
“I aged out of pediatric care at 19 with no plan. Nobody told me where to go next. The TRI-CNS assessment helped my new doctor understand exactly where I was — not just medically, but in my life.”

The A2A Transition Readiness Index gives clinicians a structured way to assess what each young adult actually needs during the handoff — not just tumor status, but cognitive function, independence, and psychosocial support.

The brain fog
“I couldn't remember my class schedule. I'd lose words mid-sentence. Everyone said I should be grateful to be alive — but I was grieving the brain I used to have.”

A2A's longitudinal neurocognitive research tracks memory, processing speed, and executive function across decades — building the evidence base for interventions that protect the developing brain during and after treatment.

The peer group
“The first time someone said 'me too' about the weird grief of survivorship — not cancer grief, but the grief of who you were before — I finally exhaled.”

A2A peer groups are led by trained young adult survivors. No agendas. No forced positivity. Just a space where you can say the hard thing out loud and know you're not the only one.

The research

From data to real change in how survivors are cared for.

Our work doesn't stay in journals. It becomes the tools clinicians use, the guidelines organizations adopt, and the interventions that protect young brains.

2028 Milestone

TRI-CNS: The first of its kind

The A2A Transition Readiness Index for CNS Tumors became the first validated, condition-specific tool for assessing whether a young adult is ready for the leap from pediatric to adult care. It's now in use at 40+ sites across the globe.

2029 Next

Protecting the developing brain

We're launching a prospective trial of a neuroprotective intervention — informed by two decades of neuroimaging and neurocognitive data — aimed at reducing radiation-related cognitive decline in young survivors.

Ongoing

Setting the global standard

A2A research shapes the Society for Neuro-Oncology AYA guidelines and the international standards of care — making sure what we learn reaches every clinic, everywhere.

Who's behind this

A clinician who chose to build what didn't exist.

Photo: Jessica D. Schulte, MD, PhD

Jessica D. Schulte, MD, PhD

Founding Director, A2A · Director, AYA Neuro-Oncology, NYU Langone Brain & Spine Tumor Center

Dr. Schulte is a neuro-oncologist who sees patients and runs a research program — because she believes you can't study survivorship without sitting across from the people living it. Her clinical focus spans late adolescence through adulthood, specializing in brain tumors, neurofibromatosis, and function-sparing, molecularly targeted therapies.

As global study chair for the PNOC long-term outcome study — tracking neurocognition and quality of life across 42 sites for up to 20 years post-diagnosis — she built the evidence base that made the TRI-CNS possible. She helped shape the 2025 SNO Guidelines for AYA care, co-chairs the SNO AYA Special Interest Track, and pioneered peer-led support groups for young adult survivors.

She received her MD and PhD from Northwestern, completed neurology residency at Columbia and neuro-oncology fellowship at UCSF, and joined NYU Langone in 2025 to direct the AYA program. A2A is the natural extension of that work: translating what she learns at the bedside into a platform that reaches every young adult who needs it.
