Adolescent to Adult Neuro-Oncology

Research that bridges the gap from survivorship to thriving.

A2A advances the science of transition, neurocognition, and quality of life for adolescents and young adults with brain and spinal cord tumors—so every survivor can live fully.

Our breakthrough

Our mission

Close the transition gap. Protect the developing brain. Extend thriving survivorship.

Adolescents and young adults with CNS tumors face a critical gap: moving from pediatric to adult care often means fragmented follow-up, lost data, and unmet neurocognitive and psychosocial needs. A2A exists to change that.

Transition science

Evidence-based protocols and tools for seamless handoff from pediatric to adult neuro-oncology, so no survivor falls through the cracks.

Neurocognition & QOL

Longitudinal research on cognition, quality of life, and functional independence—up to 20 years post-diagnosis—to guide function-sparing care.

Peer-led support

Novel peer-led support models for AYA survivors, designed with and for young adults navigating life after a brain or spine tumor.

2028 breakthrough

The A2A Transition Readiness Index—and what's next.

After years of leadership in AYA neuro-oncology and as global study chair of the PNOC long-term outcome study, our founding director led the team that delivered the first validated, condition-specific transition readiness instrument for CNS tumor survivors. It's now in use across 40+ sites. We're on the verge of the next leap.

2028: TRI-CNS

The A2A Transition Readiness Index for CNS Tumors (TRI-CNS) became the first psychometrically validated tool for assessing transition readiness in brain and spine tumor survivors—addressing a gap identified in systematic reviews and now adopted in SNO-aligned AYA care pathways.

2029: Predictive neuroprotection

We're weeks from announcing a prospective trial of a neuroprotective intervention informed by our longitudinal neuroimaging and neurocognitive data—aimed at mitigating radiation-related cognitive decline in AYA survivors.

Global standards

Our work continues to shape the Society for Neuro-Oncology AYA guidelines and the AYA neuro-oncology special interest track, ensuring research translates into standards of care worldwide.

Founding director

Leadership rooted in AYA neuro-oncology

Photo: Jessica D. Schulte, MD, PhD

Jessica D. Schulte, MD, PhD

Founding Director, A2A · Director, Adolescent & Young Adult Neuro-Oncology, NYU Langone Brain & Spine Tumor Center

Dr. Schulte is a neuro-oncologist whose clinical and research focus spans late adolescence through adulthood. She specializes in primary and metastatic brain tumors, neurofibromatosis, and schwannomatosis, with a steadfast commitment to quality of life and function-sparing, molecularly targeted therapies for AYA patients.

She is global study chair for the Pediatric Neuro-Oncology Consortium (PNOC) long-term outcome study, which collects unparalleled longitudinal data on neurocognition, quality of life, and functional independence in CNS tumor patients—up to 20 years post-diagnosis. She helped shape the 2025 SNO Guidelines for AYA neuro-oncology care and co-chairs the 2026–2027 SNO Special Interest Track for AYA Neuro-Oncology. She has pioneered peer-led young adult support groups and continues to see patients at NYU Langone while leading A2A's research mission.

She received her MD and PhD from Northwestern University, completed neurology residency at Columbia and neuro-oncology fellowship at UCSF, and joined NYU Langone in 2025 to direct the AYA Neuro-Oncology program.
